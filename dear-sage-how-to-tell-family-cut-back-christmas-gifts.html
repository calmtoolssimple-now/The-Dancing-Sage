You're buying for 23 people and it's got out of hand. How to bring it up without looking cheap.

A question the Sage hears often

Every year our family Christmas gets bigger and more expensive. Between the grown kids, their partners, the grandchildren and my sisters' families, I'm buying for 23 people. I'd love to cut back, but I don't want to look cheap or spoil anyone's Christmas. How do I bring it up?

In short: Bring it up now, before the shopping starts, and offer a specific plan rather than simply "less". You'll very likely find that most of the family is relieved.

The Sage replies…

You're almost certainly not the only one

In nearly every big family, someone else is quietly dreading the list too. They just don't want to be the one to say it. Once you do, don't be surprised if you hear a chorus of "Oh, thank goodness."

Bring it up now, not in December

Timing is everything. Once people have started buying, changing the plan feels awkward and somebody ends up with a pile of presents and nothing to give in return. October or early November is perfect.

Offer a plan, not just a cut

"Let's spend less" leaves everyone guessing. A clear suggestion is much easier to agree to. A few that work well:

Secret Santa for the grown-ups, with a spending limit, and proper presents for the children.
Children only: adults give each other nothing, or a card.
One gift per household, rather than per person.
A day together instead, like a skate, a show or a big brunch.
Homemade or edible gifts only: cookies, jam, a knitted scarf.

What to say

A family dinner or the family group chat both work. Keep it warm and simple:

<em>"I love our Christmases, but buying for everyone has got a bit out of hand for me. How would everyone feel about a Secret Santa for the grown-ups this year, with a $40 limit, and keeping proper presents for the kids?"</em>

Notice it's about you, not anyone else's spending. Nobody has to feel judged.

If someone isn't keen

Some people truly love giving, and that's fine. You're changing what <em>you</em> do, not policing everyone else. If they still give you something, accept it graciously. Keep a small box of chocolates or a jar of something homemade on hand, just in case.
