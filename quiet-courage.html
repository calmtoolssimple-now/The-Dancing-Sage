Quiet Courage

A reflection on the kind of courage that doesn't announce itself, and why it counts just as much.

← Back to the Wisdom Library

In short: Most of the courage in a woman's life is never seen by anyone. It's getting up on heavy mornings, keeping promises to yourself and saying the honest thing in a quiet voice. If today asked more of you than it looked like from the outside, that was courage, and it counts.

We tend to picture courage as something dramatic. A leap. A declaration. A moment someone will remember and retell.

But most of the courage in a woman's life is never witnessed by anyone at all.

What quiet courage looks like

It is the courage to get up on the mornings that feel heavier than the ones before. To keep a promise to yourself that no one is checking on. To say the honest thing in a quiet voice instead of swallowing it again.

It is choosing, one more time, not to disappear inside what everyone else needs from you.

This kind of courage rarely gets applause. It doesn't make a good story. It simply gets you through the door, through the day, through the version of yourself that was harder to be than the world will ever know.

Strength without a scene

There is a particular strength in doing something hard without making a scene of it. In carrying a weight and still setting the table. In feeling afraid and choosing forward anyway, quietly, without needing anyone to notice the choosing.

If today required more from you than it looked like from the outside, that was courage. It counted. It still counts, even now, even unseen.

You do not need a dramatic moment to prove your strength. You have already been proving it, gently, in ways too small for anyone else to measure.

— The Dancing Sage

Try this in everyday life

Rehearse the first sentence

A difficult conversation often feels larger before its opening words have taken shape.

Write one sentence you want to say. Keep it about a clear request or experience: I would like a turn to choose our weekend plans; I need more notice before I can help; I would like to join you, although I am new to this. Read it aloud once in an ordinary voice. You do not need to make it sound impressive.

Choose a manageable setting for saying it, or practise with someone you trust first. You can also write a message when speaking feels too demanding. Courage can include seeking support, changing your plan or deciding that a situation is not safe for a direct conversation. Being quiet should never mean having to endure mistreatment alone.

A question to take with you: What is the smallest honest sentence I have been postponing?
