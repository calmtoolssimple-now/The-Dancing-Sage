A question the Sage hears often

Every Christmas I make the gravy last, everyone is hovering, and every year it comes out lumpy. My sister-in-law makes a face. How do I fix it, and how do I stop it happening in the first place?

In short: Lumps happen when flour hits hot liquid all at once. Mix the flour into cold water first, or cook it in the fat before you add the broth slowly. Already lumpy? Pour it through a sieve or give it a ten-second blitz. Best of all, make it a few days ahead.

The Sage replies…

First, it isn't you. It's the flour.

Flour dropped into hot liquid cooks on the outside before the inside gets wet, and you get little dumplings. Every gravy disaster in history comes down to that.

The two-minute rescue

The sieve: pour the gravy through a fine sieve into a warm jug and press the lumps through with a spoon. Done.

The blitz: ten seconds with a hand blender, right in the pot. Silky, and nobody will ever know.

Too thin after all that? Stir a spoonful of cornstarch into a little cold water and whisk it in while it simmers.

The never-again method

Pour off a few spoonfuls of fat from the roasting pan. Whisk in the same amount of flour and let it bubble for a minute or two until it smells toasty. Then add warm broth a ladle at a time, whisking like you mean it, until it's as thick as you like. Slow and steady wins.

The real secret: make it ahead

Roast a few chicken wings with an onion and carrot, add water, simmer, strain, and make your gravy a week early. It freezes beautifully. On the day, just warm it and stir in the turkey drippings for flavour. One less job while everyone is hovering.

And your sister-in-law?

Hand her the whisk next year. Funny how the faces stop when people have a job to do.
