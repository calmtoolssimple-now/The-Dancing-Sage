Let Joy Be Simple

Joy does not always arrive as fireworks. Sometimes it is toast, tea, sunlight, and a quiet room.

We are taught to wait for joy the way we wait for a holiday — something scheduled, something earned, something that arrives with enough fanfare to prove it was worth the wait.

But most joy does not actually arrive that way.

Where joy actually lives

It arrives as steam curling off a mug in a quiet kitchen. As a favorite song playing at exactly the right volume in the car. As sunlight moving slowly across a wall while you do absolutely nothing important at all.

These moments rarely feel significant while they're happening. They pass by disguised as ordinary, easy to overlook if you're busy waiting for the bigger version of happiness you assume is coming later.

The pressure of "memorable"

But joy that requires a special occasion is joy you'll only feel a few times a year. Joy that lives in the ordinary is available almost constantly, if you're willing to actually notice it instead of scrolling past it toward the next thing.

There is nothing lesser about small joy. A slice of buttered toast eaten slowly, with attention, can hold as much genuine contentment as any grand celebration — sometimes more, because it doesn't come with the pressure of being memorable.

"Let joy be simple. It was probably trying to reach you that way all along." — The Dancing Sage  ·  Share by email

Today's small joy

Today, look for the small version instead of waiting for the big one. The warm cup. The soft light. The five quiet minutes before anyone needs anything from you.

— The Dancing Sage 🌿

Try this in everyday life

Build a three-choice joy menu

A short menu can be more useful than a long list of things you ought to enjoy.

Write one quiet choice, one creative choice and one connection choice. For example: tea by the window; ten minutes colouring a flower; a voice message to a friend. Beside each, note what you need and how long it might take. Use things you already have before adding anything to a shopping list.

Try one choice today, then record a simple response: enjoyed it, not today, or would change something. If colouring felt fiddly, choose larger shapes. If a phone call was too much, send a short message. The menu belongs to you, so it can change with your energy, interests and circumstances. Joy is an invitation here, not another thing to perform well.

A question to take with you: Which small pleasure would I choose again because I liked it, not because it was good for my image?
