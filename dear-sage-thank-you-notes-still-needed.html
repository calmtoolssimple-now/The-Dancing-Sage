A question the Sage hears often

I was raised to write thank-you notes for everything. My grandchildren never send them, and my daughter says a text is fine these days. Am I old-fashioned, or should I still expect a note?

In short: A heartfelt text is fine for everyday kindnesses. A handwritten note is still best for gifts sent by mail, wedding and shower presents, sympathy, and anyone who went truly out of their way.

The Sage replies…

You're not old-fashioned; you're gracious

A thank-you note is never wrong. It is one of the few small things in life that costs very little and is remembered for years. People keep handwritten notes in drawers. Nobody keeps a text in a drawer.

That said, manners do change with the times, and the heart of the thing matters more than the paper. A warm, specific message sent promptly is better than a beautiful card that never gets written.

When a text is perfectly fine

A quick message is lovely for everyday kindnesses: a dinner invitation, a favour, a lift to the airport, a small gift handed over in person and thanked for on the spot. Make it specific: "Thank you for dinner last night. That lemon cake! I'm still thinking about it."

When a note is still best

A handwritten note is still the gracious choice for wedding and shower gifts, presents that arrived by mail (so the sender knows they arrived), sympathy and condolences, and anyone who went well out of their way for you. It's also a lovely gift to give someone older, who will treasure it.

As for the grandchildren: rather than expecting notes, teach them. Buy them a pack of pretty cards and stamps, sit with them, and make it fun. Model it, and praise them when they do it. Manners are caught more than taught.

With a wink and a twirl,
— The Dancing Sage

Try this

A three-line thank-you note

Line 1: thank them for the specific thing. Line 2: say how you'll use it or how it made you feel. Line 3: a warm wish for them. That's all a good note needs. Write one this week to someone who won't be expecting it.

A question to take with you: Who has been quietly kind to me this year without ever being thanked?
