A question the Sage hears often

I see my grandchildren a few times a year, and at every family dinner they sit there with their phones. Their parents don't say anything. I don't want to be the cranky old lady, but it hurts. What can I do?

In short: Don't scold, invite. Talk to the parents beforehand, set up a friendly 'phone basket' with a small prize, give the kids a job at the table, and be the most interesting thing in the room. A lecture loses to a screen every time. Fun wins.

The Sage replies…

It hurts because you miss them

Let's say that first, because it's true. You're not being old-fashioned. You just want to see their faces, not the tops of their heads.

Have a quiet word with the parents first

Not a complaint, a plan. Something like, "I'd love a phone-free dinner this year. Would you back me up?" Kids follow the grown-ups, so if Mom and Dad pocket their phones too, half the battle is won.

Make it a game, not a rule

The phone basket: a pretty basket by the door. Whoever leaves theirs there all through dinner picks first from the dessert table, or wins a toonie. Teenagers will do almost anything for a dare and a treat.

Give them a job: place cards, pouring drinks, carving duty for the oldest, the cracker jokes for the youngest. Busy hands don't scroll.

Bring a conversation jar: slips of paper with questions like "What's the worst food you've ever tasted?" or "What would you do with a million dollars?" Questions about them, not about school.

Then make a deal

After dessert, ask them to show you something on their phone. A funny video, the game they love, how to send you a selfie. You'll be amazed how fast a teenager warms up when they get to be the expert.

The grumpy grandma test

If you hear yourself saying "In my day…", stop. If you hear yourself laughing, you're winning.
