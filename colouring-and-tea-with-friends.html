Colouring & Tea with Friends

Plan a relaxed colouring-and-tea afternoon with an easy invitation, simple supplies and conversation starters.

By The Dancing Sage · About this website

A colouring-and-tea afternoon can be as simple as two people, two cups and a few pencils. The activity gives your hands something pleasant to do while the conversation finds its own pace. You do not need matching books, a beautifully styled room or a reason to celebrate.

This plan is for a small, informal gathering at home. Use the shorter version for a first invitation, and adjust everything to the people coming.

Invite one person to begin

A specific, modest invitation is easier to answer than a vague promise to get together. Try: “Would you like to come over for tea and a little colouring on Thursday at two? About an hour, very casual. Bring your pencils if you like, but I have some to share.”

Let your friend know that colouring is optional. They may prefer to watch, bring knitting or simply talk. If you are meeting online, agree on a time and let each person prepare their own drink and supplies. No one needs to print the same picture.

Set out a small, comfortable space

  • Clear enough table space for each page and a resting hand.
  • Put out a few pencils in a jar or shallow dish, plus a sharpener.
  • Offer pages with different sizes of spaces, or plain paper for doodling.
  • Keep drinks on coasters beyond the working area.
  • Check the light and choose seating your guest finds comfortable.

Ask what your guest likes to drink instead of assuming tea is essential. A favourite cup can make an ordinary drink feel special, but an everyday mug belongs here too. Snacks are optional. A clear table and a warm welcome are enough preparation.

A gentle one-hour plan

  1. First 10 minutes: make drinks, catch up and look through the pages.
  2. Next 10 minutes: choose a few colours and begin with a large, easy shape.
  3. Next 25 minutes: colour and chat, or enjoy a little comfortable silence.
  4. Last 15 minutes: finish your drinks, admire a colour you liked and put the supplies away. Nobody needs to complete the picture.

If an hour is too much, make it a half-hour visit: five minutes to settle, twenty minutes together and five minutes to finish. If the conversation takes over and the pencils barely move, the afternoon has still done what you hoped.

Conversation starters that leave room for lightness

  • Did you have a favourite colour when you were little?
  • What is one small thing you have enjoyed this week?
  • Is there a craft you would like to try again?
  • Which room, garden or place would you like to borrow these colours from?
  • Does this cup remind you of anyone or anywhere?

You do not need to work through the questions as a list. Pick one if conversation stalls, and let the answer lead somewhere unexpected. Leave room for private subjects to stay private. Ask before photographing your guest or sharing their page online.

Make room for different preferences

Offer larger shapes if small details are tiring. Let someone bring supplies they already find comfortable. Keep music low enough for easy conversation, or leave it off. Put out a spare page so a guest can test colours without marking the picture they chose.

Avoid turning the visit into a lesson unless someone has asked for help. “I like those two colours together” is often more welcoming than correcting how a pencil is held or suggesting a better shade. Each person can decide how much advice they want.

A cup with a story

If you enjoy special teacups, choose one for the afternoon and share its story: a charity-shop discovery, a family piece or simply a pattern you liked. A new cup is not necessary for each visit. Reusing a favourite can become part of the pleasure.

You might also choose an optional colour theme inspired by the cup. Blue and white, rose and green, or cream and gold can give everyone a starting point while leaving plenty of room for their own choices.

End with an invitation, not an obligation

Ask what your friend enjoyed and whether they would like to do it again. Agree on another date only if it suits you both. Keep the setup easy enough that hosting does not become a chore.
