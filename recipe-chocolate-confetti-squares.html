Chocolate Confetti Squares

Don't love baking, but still want to impress everyone at Christmas? Darling, these are for you. Four ingredients, no oven, and if everyone thinks you've been baking all afternoon, there's absolutely no reason to correct them.

These old-fashioned chocolate confetti squares (some families call them peanut butter marshmallow squares or rainbow squares) have been a Canadian Christmas tray favourite for generations. The coloured mini marshmallows are the "confetti."

  • No oven
  • 15 min + chilling
  • Makes 36 small squares

Ingredients

  • ¼ cup butter or margarine
  • ½ cup peanut butter
  • 1 cup semisweet chocolate chips
  • 1 bag (250 g) miniature coloured marshmallows

Instructions

  1. Melt the butter and peanut butter together in a large saucepan over low heat.
  2. Stir in the chocolate chips until completely melted and smooth.
  3. Remove from the heat and let the mixture cool until the bottom of the saucepan is comfortable to touch.
  4. Gently fold in the coloured marshmallows until completely coated.
  5. Press into a parchment-lined 9 × 9-inch pan.
  6. Refrigerate until firm, then cut into squares.
🌿 Sage's Tip

Make sure the chocolate mixture has cooled before adding the marshmallows. Otherwise, you'll end up with chocolate marshmallow soup!

Good to know

Storing

Keep the squares in an airtight container in the fridge for up to a week. They soften at room temperature, so bring them out about 10 minutes before serving and put leftovers back in the fridge.

Freezing

Confetti squares freeze beautifully. Layer them between sheets of parchment in a freezer container and freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw in the fridge, and they taste just as good as the day you made them.

Swaps

For a nut-free version, use sunflower seed butter instead of peanut butter. Many families make these with butterscotch chips instead of chocolate chips, which is the old-fashioned way and just as good. White marshmallows work fine if you can't find the coloured ones, but the colours are half the fun.

Cutting neat squares

Lift the whole slab out of the pan using the parchment, then cut with a large knife dipped in hot water and wiped dry between cuts. Chilled squares cut cleanly; warm ones squish.

Questions people ask

Why did my marshmallows melt?

The chocolate mixture was still too hot. Let it cool until the bottom of the saucepan feels comfortable to touch before you fold in the marshmallows, and stir gently.

Can I make Chocolate Confetti Squares ahead for Christmas?

Yes. They keep for a week in the fridge or up to 3 months in the freezer, which makes them one of the easiest squares to get done early.

Do I have to use a 9 × 9 pan?

An 8 × 8 pan gives you thicker squares, and that works too. A 9 × 13 pan makes them too thin, so double the recipe if you want to use one.
