Gathering the Scattered Pieces

Healing often begins when we stop chasing every piece and simply sit with what is here.

← Back to the Wisdom Library

After something breaks, there is often an urge to collect every piece immediately, to reassemble the whole thing before anyone notices it fell apart at all.

But healing rarely works on that kind of schedule.

Some pieces will still be scattered a long way off, in places you haven't even thought to look yet — an old memory, a habit you didn't realize you built as protection, a fear that still flinches at something that reminds it of the break. You cannot gather what you haven't found. And you cannot find everything at once.

This is not failure. This is simply how healing actually moves.

Imagine someone kneeling in a garden after a storm, gathering broken stems one at a time instead of trying to hold the entire mess in two hands at once. They do not need to fix the whole garden in an afternoon. They only need to pick up what is nearest, and set it somewhere safe, and come back tomorrow for more.

You are allowed to heal like that. Slowly. Unevenly. One piece at a time, without needing the whole picture restored before you'll let yourself feel okay.

Today, you do not need to gather everything. You only need to pick up the piece that is closest to you right now, and hold it gently, and trust that the rest will still be there when you are ready for it.

— The Dancing Sage
