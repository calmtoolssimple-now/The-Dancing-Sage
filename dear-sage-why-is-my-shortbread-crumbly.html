A question the Sage hears often

My shortbread tastes lovely, but it falls apart the moment anyone picks it up, and sometimes the dough won't even hold together to roll. My grandmother's always kept its shape. What am I doing wrong?

In short: Usually it's too much flour, butter that's too cold, or baking a little too long. Weigh the flour, use properly softened butter, knead briefly until the dough holds, and bake only until pale gold.

The Sage replies…

Tender is good, crumbly isn't

Good shortbread should be tender and melt in your mouth, but still hold together in your hand. Because there are only three ingredients, small changes make a big difference. The classic proportion is 1 part sugar, 2 parts butter and 3 parts flour, by weight.

The usual culprits

Too much flour. Scooping straight from the bag packs in far more than the recipe expects. Spoon it into the cup and level it, or better still, weigh it.

Butter that's too cold. If the butter isn't soft enough to cream with the sugar, the dough never quite binds. It should hold a fingerprint easily, without looking greasy.

Too little mixing. Shortbread needs a short knead at the end to bring it together. Just until it's smooth, no longer.

Baking too long. Shortbread should be pale gold, not brown. A few extra minutes dries it out.

Cutting it cold. If you bake it in a pan, cut it into fingers while it's still warm. Cold shortbread shatters.

How to rescue crumbly dough

Don't throw it out. Knead it for a minute with warm hands, which often does the trick. If it's still dry, work in a teaspoon of soft butter at a time until it holds. Then press it into the pan instead of rolling it, if rolling is a struggle.

A few of Grandma's secrets

Use icing sugar for a finer, more delicate texture. Swap a few spoonfuls of the flour for rice flour or cornstarch to get that melt-in-the-mouth crumb. And chill the shaped shortbread for 20 minutes before baking so it keeps its shape.

Weigh it once

Next batch, weigh your flour instead of using a cup, even just once. You may be amazed at how much extra you've been adding.
