A little planning should give you more time to enjoy Christmas - not give you another job to manage.

Most Christmas stress isn't the work itself. It's the remembering: who's coming, who's bringing what, whether anyone told Auntie Joan about the nut allergy, and why you're the only one who knows where the gravy boat lives. This planner takes the remembering out of your head and puts it somewhere the whole family can see it.

What's inside the planner

There are nine tabs. Use the ones that help and ignore the rest. Fill in the cream-coloured cells, and the totals and checks work themselves out.

Start HereA one-page guide to how the planner works, so nobody in the family needs a tutorial.
Family Christmas HubSecret Santa, the spending limit, key dates and dinner details, all agreed in one place.
Guest ListWho's coming, adults and children, arrival times and allergies. Only "Yes" guests count in the totals.
Potluck PlannerWho's bringing which dish. A summary tells you whether mains, sides, salads and desserts are covered for your number of guests.
Shared DutiesEveryone gets a real job, from drinks and ice to the dishes. Print it and stick it on the fridge.
Day PlanType in your dinner time and it works backwards, telling you when to start the main dish, set the table and warm the rolls.
SuppliesChairs, plates, cutlery, serving spoons and fridge space, checked a week ahead so there's time to borrow.
Baking PlanTell it how many people and gatherings you're baking for, and it tells you whether you've planned enough or can simplify.
Cookie ExchangeSet the date and how many cookies each guest takes home, and it tells every baker exactly how many dozen to bring.

How to use it in four steps

  1. Download and save a copy to your computer before you start filling it in.
  2. Share it with the family. Upload it to Google Sheets or OneDrive and send the link, so people can add their own dish and arrival time instead of texting you.
  3. Make the big decisions first on the Family Christmas Hub: who's hosting, the gift plan and the dinner time.
  4. Print the Shared Duties tab a few days before and put it somewhere everyone will see it.

A calm Christmas timeline

You don't need to start in September. You just need to do the right things in the right order, so the last week is for enjoying, not scrambling.

Early November: the big decisions. Who's hosting, who's invited, Secret Santa or not, and the spending limit. These are the conversations that cause trouble when left until December.
Mid November: the potluck and the jobs. Share the planner, let people choose their dishes, and hand out the jobs while everyone is still feeling generous.
Late November: baking that freezes. Shortbread, gingerbread dough and most squares freeze beautifully. Pick a short list and bake a little at a time.
Early December: gifts and the cookie exchange. Finish most of the shopping, and hold the cookie exchange in the first or second weekend so the cookies are there for the rest of the month.
One week before: supplies and the fridge. Run down the Supplies tab, borrow chairs, and clear fridge space for everyone else's dishes.
The day before: set the table. Set the table, pull frozen baking out to thaw, and set your dinner time on the Day Plan.
Christmas Day: follow the plan. Let the Day Plan tell you what's next, let the people on the duty list do their jobs, and sit down.
The Sage's potluck rule of thumb: for about 20 people you need one main dish, two or three vegetable sides, one potato dish, one salad, rolls, two or three desserts and the drinks. Any more than that is just more dishes to wash.

Three gentle rules for a calmer Christmas

1. Everyone gets a real job

Not "bring a bag of buns", but a job that matters: carving, coffee, dishes, entertaining the little ones. People enjoy the day more when they're part of it, and you get to sit down.

2. Decide early, then stop deciding

Most holiday tension comes from decisions left hanging. Settle the gift limit, the guest list and the dinner time early, write them in the Hub, and you won't need to talk about them again.

3. Plan less than you think

If the Baking Plan says you have plenty, believe it. Five lovely things are better than twelve made at midnight. Nobody will remember the seventh kind of cookie, but they will remember whether you were laughing.

Questions people ask

Do I need Microsoft Excel?

No. The planner is an .xlsx file, which also opens in Google Sheets (free with a Google account), Apple Numbers and LibreOffice. The automatic totals work in all of them.

Can the whole family use it at once?

Yes. Upload it to Google Sheets or OneDrive and share the link. Everyone can add their own dish, job or arrival time.
