A question the Sage hears often

I was making an apple pie the other day and my grandmother told me I should serve it with a slice of cheddar cheese. Is that a real thing? I have to admit, I was a bit grossed out.

In short: It's very real, and very old. Sharp cheddar with apple pie is a long-standing tradition in parts of England, New England and here in Canada. The salty, tangy cheese balances the sweet apples. Try a small thin slice of old cheddar before you decide.

Your grandmother knows her stuff

Apple pie with cheddar has been served for generations. In Yorkshire there's an old saying that an apple pie without the cheese is like a kiss without the squeeze. In New England, Vermont especially, a wedge of sharp cheddar beside the pie is a classic, and plenty of Canadian grandmothers grew up with it too.

Why it works

Think of a cheese board with apple slices, or a ploughman's lunch with chutney. Sweet fruit and sharp, salty cheese are old friends. The cheddar cuts through the sugar, so each bite tastes less rich and more interesting. It's the same reason salted caramel is so popular.

Three ways to try it

The classic: a thin slice of sharp, aged cheddar on the side of a warm slice of pie.

The melty one: lay a slice of cheddar on top of the warm pie for the last few minutes in the oven, until it just softens.

The baker's secret: grate a handful of sharp cheddar into your pastry. It makes a golden, savoury crust that's wonderful with apples.

Choose the right cheese

Pick an old or extra-old cheddar, the sharper the better. Mild cheddar just tastes like a sandwich. And serve it at room temperature, since cold cheese hides its flavour.

And if you still don't like it?

Then serve Grandma her cheddar and enjoy yours with ice cream. Traditions are meant to be shared, not forced. But do try one small bite first. You may find yourself fighting her for the last slice.

Apple Pie With Cheddar Cheese: The Recipe

A classic double-crust apple pie with sharp cheddar baked right into the pastry, plus more on the side. Makes one 9-inch pie, about 8 slices.

Prep: 30 minutes, plus 1 hour to chill the pastry · Bake: about 1 hour · Cool: 2 hours

For the cheddar pastry

  • 2½ cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup cold butter, cut into small cubes
  • 1 cup finely grated old or extra-old cheddar
  • 6 to 8 tablespoons ice water

For the filling

  • 6 to 7 medium apples (about 2½ lb), peeled, cored and sliced ¼ inch thick. A mix of tart and sweet is best, such as Northern Spy, Granny Smith, Cortland or Honeycrisp.
  • ½ cup white sugar
  • ¼ cup packed brown sugar
  • 3 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon cinnamon
  • ¼ teaspoon nutmeg
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • A pinch of salt
  • 1 tablespoon butter, cut in small bits
  • 1 egg beaten with 1 tablespoon milk, for brushing

To serve

  • A wedge of old or extra-old cheddar, at room temperature

Steps

  1. Make the pastry. Stir the flour, sugar and salt together. Rub or cut in the cold butter until it looks like coarse crumbs with some pea-sized bits. Stir in the grated cheddar. Sprinkle in ice water a tablespoon at a time, tossing with a fork, until the dough just holds together when squeezed.
  2. Chill. Divide the dough in two, press each half into a flat disc, wrap and refrigerate for at least 1 hour.
  3. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C), with a rack in the lower third. Put a baking sheet underneath to catch drips.
  4. Mix the filling. Toss the apple slices with both sugars, flour, cinnamon, nutmeg, lemon juice and salt.
  5. Fill the pie. Roll one disc of pastry into a 12-inch circle and line a 9-inch pie plate. Pile in the apples, mounding them in the middle, and dot with the butter. Roll out the second disc, lay it on top, trim, then fold and crimp the edges to seal.
  6. Vent and brush. Cut 4 or 5 slits in the top crust and brush it with the egg wash.
  7. Bake. Bake at 425°F for 20 minutes, then lower the heat to 375°F (190°C) and bake another 35 to 45 minutes, until the crust is deep golden and the juices bubble through the slits. If the edges brown too fast, cover them with strips of foil.
  8. Cool. Let the pie cool for at least 2 hours so the filling sets. Cut too soon and it will run.
  9. Serve with cheddar. Serve each slice with a thin slice of sharp cheddar on the side. Or, for the melty version, lay cheddar on slices of warm pie and pop them into a 350°F oven for 3 to 5 minutes, until the cheese just softens.

Questions people ask

What kind of cheese goes with apple pie? A sharp, aged cheddar, labelled old or extra-old in Canada. Mild cheddar is too plain to stand up to the sweet filling.

Is there a name for apple pie with cheese? Not really. Most people just call it apple pie with cheddar. When the cheese is baked into the pastry, it's usually called a cheddar-crust apple pie.

Can I make it ahead? Yes. The pastry keeps in the fridge for 2 days, and the baked pie keeps at room temperature for a day or in the fridge for up to 4 days. Warm slices gently before adding the cheese.
