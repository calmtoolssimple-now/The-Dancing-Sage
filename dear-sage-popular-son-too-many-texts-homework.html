Dear Dancing Sage,

My son has several girls calling and texting him constantly! I know he's popular, but all this attention is distracting him from school. Popular or not, he has to study! What should I do? And what happens when I ask him to put the phone away and he pushes back?

— Mum of Mr. Popular

In short: He doesn't need to be less popular, just less available during homework. Set a specific homework time with the phone in the kitchen, keep your voice steady when he objects, follow through calmly, and ask what's making it hard.

The Sage replies…

Dear Mum,

Ah, young love. Apparently it now comes with unlimited notifications.

Your son doesn't need to become less popular. He needs to become less available during homework. There's room in his life for friendship, a little romance, and the occasional quadratic equation. The challenge is convincing him that all three don't need his attention at precisely the same moment.

Start with a conversation when neither of you is already annoyed. Trying to introduce a new household rule while his phone is buzzing and your patience is disappearing rarely brings out anyone's finest qualities.

Keep it straightforward: "I'm glad you have friends you enjoy talking to. But your schoolwork needs your attention, too. We need a routine that makes room for both."

Give him a timeline

"Start your homework at six. We'll check what's finished at seven. During that time, the phone stays in the kitchen."

Make the expectation specific. "Study sometime tonight" leaves a remarkable amount of room for interpretation, especially when someone interesting has just sent a heart emoji.

If he needs his phone for an assignment, ask him to show you what he needs and agree on how he'll use it. If the work takes longer than expected, check whether he needs help or a short break.

Keep the conversation about his responsibilities. The girls aren't in charge of his homework—he is. Blaming them may make him defensive and distract everyone from the actual issue.

And remind him: "Anyone worth your time can wait while you finish the math homework."

When Mr. Popular objects

"You can't tell me what to do!" may arrive before the first textbook opens.

Resist the invitation to a shouting match. You don't have to win an argument about whether you are the world's strictest mother. You need a workable homework routine.

Try: "I hear that you're frustrated. The expectation is still homework first, chatting afterward."

Keep your voice steady and your explanation short. Repeating yourself twelve different ways generally supplies twelve new things to argue about.

He can be disappointed. He can disagree. He can express both respectfully. You can listen without changing the boundary simply because he dislikes it.

If voices rise, pause: "We'll talk again in ten minutes when we're calmer." Then return to the conversation.

Follow through—and stay curious

Agree beforehand on a reasonable consequence: recreational phone use waits until the agreed schoolwork is finished. Choose something you can enforce calmly and consistently.

"No phone until you're thirty" may feel satisfying, but it creates certain administrative difficulties.

Also ask what's making this hard. Is the assignment confusing? Is he overwhelmed? Does he worry someone will lose interest if he doesn't answer immediately? Listen carefully; the answer may reveal a problem that needs help alongside the boundary.

Once he gets started, notice it: "Thanks for putting your phone away and getting on with it." Recognition doesn't need a parade.

As he becomes more dependable, give him opportunities to manage the routine himself. You're helping him practise a skill he'll need at college, at work, and in relationships: being present for the thing in front of him.

Popularity is lovely, Mum. Learning when to silence the notifications will serve him even longer.

Warmly,
— The Dancing Sage 🌿
