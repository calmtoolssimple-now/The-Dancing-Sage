Coming Home to Yourself

Home is not always a place. Sometimes it is the moment we stop abandoning ourselves.

← Back to the Wisdom Library

For years, many of us learn to leave ourselves quietly, one small compromise at a time.

We shrink an opinion to keep the peace. We swallow a need because voicing it felt like too much. We hand our attention to everyone else's crisis until there's nothing left over for our own. Each moment on its own feels small. Together, over years, they add up to a kind of quiet self-abandonment.

Coming home does not always mean returning somewhere physical. More often, it means returning to yourself — noticing what you actually want again, after years of asking everyone else first. Feeling your own feelings instead of managing everyone else's. Taking up the space you quietly gave away.

This return rarely happens all at once. It happens in small, almost imperceptible moments. Choosing rest over guilt, once. Saying what you actually think, once. Letting yourself want something without immediately deciding it's selfish, once.

Each of these small returns is its own kind of homecoming.

You do not need to have it all figured out to come home to yourself. You do not need a finished, healed, fully self-actualized version of you waiting at the door. You only need to stop leaving, one small moment at a time, starting now.

Wherever you have been abandoning yourself lately, in the smallest of ways, this is your reminder that you're allowed to come back.

You are allowed to come home to yourself. Today is as good a day as any to begin.

— The Dancing Sage
