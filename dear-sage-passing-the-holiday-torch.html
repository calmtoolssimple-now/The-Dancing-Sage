A question the Sage hears often

I have hosted Christmas, Easter and Thanksgiving for my family for over twenty years. I love them all dearly, but I am worn out. Everyone just assumes it will be at my house. How do I step back without anyone feeling hurt, or thinking I don't care any more?

In short: Tell them early, tell them warmly, and frame it as a new tradition rather than an ending. Offer a clear plan, such as rotating hosts or a potluck, so nobody is left guessing.

The Sage replies…

First, darling, a standing ovation

Twenty years of turkeys, gravy, extra chairs and washing up. That is not nothing. That is a small catering company you ran for free, out of love. Before we talk about stepping back, I want you to know that wanting a rest does not mean you love them less. It means you are human.

Here is the secret most hostesses never hear: your family probably has no idea how much it takes. They see the beautiful table, not the three days of shopping and the 6 a.m. start. They are not being thoughtless. They just haven't been asked.

Say it early, and say it like good news

Don't wait until December, when everyone has already made plans around your kitchen. Raise it in the autumn, at a relaxed moment, not in the middle of a busy dinner.

Try something like: "I've loved hosting all these years, and I'd love to try something new. This year, I'd like us to share it around." Notice that this isn't an apology. It's an invitation.

Give them a plan to say yes to

People resist change when it feels vague. Make it easy by offering a choice: a different family member hosts each year, everyone brings a dish, or you keep hosting but the cooking and cleaning are shared out. You might even suggest a short family planning chat, twenty minutes over tea, to agree who does what.

If someone grumbles, smile and hold steady. "I know, it's different. I think we'll like it." You don't need to defend yourself. Traditions are allowed to grow up, just like the children did.

With a wink and a twirl,
— The Dancing Sage

Try this

Send the message this week

Pick one holiday to start with, not all of them. Write two sentences: one thanking everyone for the years of happy gatherings, and one suggesting the new arrangement. Send it, or say it, before you can talk yourself out of it.

A question to take with you: Which part of hosting do I secretly enjoy, and which part could I happily hand to someone else?
