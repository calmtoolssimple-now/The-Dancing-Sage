The Wisdom of Slow Mornings

The way we enter the morning can change the way we carry the whole day.

← Back to the Wisdom Library

The first few minutes of a day tend to set its tone far more than we give them credit for.

Rush out of bed already behind, phone in hand before your eyes have fully adjusted, and the whole day tends to inherit that same frantic energy. Begin more slowly, even by five minutes, and something in the day's pace shifts along with you.

This is not about becoming a person who wakes at dawn to meditate on a mountaintop. Most mornings don't allow for that, and pretending otherwise only adds another way to feel like you're failing before breakfast.

It is smaller than that. It is choosing not to reach for your phone in the very first minute. It is drinking your coffee sitting down, even briefly, instead of standing at the counter already three tasks deep. It is letting your body wake up gradually instead of launching it straight into demand.

A slow morning is not a luxury reserved for people with easier lives. It is a small act of self-respect available to almost anyone, if we're willing to protect even a sliver of it.

The day will ask enough of you soon. It always does. But it does not need to start asking the second your eyes open.

Tomorrow, try giving yourself five unclaimed minutes before the day begins requiring things of you. Not productive minutes. Just yours. See what it does to everything that follows.

— The Dancing Sage
